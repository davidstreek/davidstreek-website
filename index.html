An outdoor room directly off the kitchen gives this Norwich home more space for everyday living. A timber canopy shelters the sandstone terrace, creating a place to sit and dine outdoors for much of the year. Alongside it, a British larch pergola offers partial shade and supports climbing plants.

The south-facing aspect and views towards Norwich Cathedral were a major draw when the client bought the house. The steep garden was largely bare, with gravel banks and a patch of lawn outside the kitchen. The brief was to make better use of that space without the scale and expense of a full kitchen extension.

Timber sleepers retain new planting beds on the former gravel banks. Plants from the client’s previous garden were added to over the following months. Within a year, climbers had begun to fill the pergola. The larch is now weathering to silver as the planting matures.

Later additions included a bespoke shed and a two-door gatehouse. The space between the doors allows deliveries to be received while keeping the client’s three dogs secure.

The client’s ambition shaped the project from the outset. What makes it especially rewarding is how much it has changed everyday life at the house. The terrace is now somewhere to have lunch, sit with a book or spend time outside under cover, and the greenhouse gives the client another reason to be in the garden.

Created for a creative director and graphic designer, this garden building brings together three distinct spaces: a studio, a bathroom with shower, and a utility and storage room. Inside, OSB-lined walls, a built-in sofa with storage and a ground concrete floor give the studio the industrial character the client wanted. Bespoke timber cladding was milled with a sawn face and central shadow gap, creating a textured matt-black finish reminiscent of charred timber.

Heavily insulated and built to meet building regulations within permitted development, the studio takes its hot water supply from the house, reducing cost and complexity. The wider project included approximately 40 m² of porcelain paving and substantial earthworks to level the lawn. Restricted access required materials to be transferred to the rear using specialist equipment. Planting is planned as the next stage.
